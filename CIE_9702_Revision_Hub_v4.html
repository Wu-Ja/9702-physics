Wave basics

Core definitions & quantities

Wave definition

A wave is a travelling disturbance that transfers energy without net transfer of matter.

波传递能量，介质粒子在平衡位置附近振动，不随波整体移动。

Types

  • Transverse: oscillations perpendicular to direction of energy transfer.
  • Longitudinal: oscillations parallel to direction of energy transfer; compressions and rarefactions.

Wave quantities & graphs

Amplitude A · wavelength λ · frequency f · period T · speed v.

  • Displacement–time graph: one particle over time → A, T.
  • Displacement–distance graph: wave snapshot → A, λ.
  • Wave speed: follow a crest/phase point.
Use correct units: λ/m, f/Hz, T/s, v/m s⁻¹.

Phase & phase difference

How far through an oscillation?

Phase

Phase describes the stage of an oscillation within one cycle. Phase difference compares the stages of two oscillations.

相位表示振动进行到周期中的哪个位置；相位差比较两个振动的状态差。
  • In phase: 0°, 360°…
  • Antiphase: 180° (π rad)

Phase difference relations

Δφ = 2π(Δt/T) = 2π(Δx/λ) rad
Δφ = 360°(Δt/T) = 360°(Δx/λ)

Δt: time shift; Δx: separation along wave; T: period; λ: wavelength.

Exam checkIf Δx=λ/4, phase difference = 90° = π/2 rad.

Superposition & interference

Two waves overlap

Principle of superposition

When two or more waves overlap, the resultant displacement at a point is the algebraic sum of their individual displacements.

y = y₁ + y₂ (+ y₃…)
重叠时位移相加；波通过后仍继续传播。

Conditions for stable interference

  • Coherent sources: same frequency and constant phase difference.
  • For light, same polarisation supports clear interference.
  • Similar amplitudes give high-contrast fringes.

Constructive interference

Waves arrive in phase; resultant amplitude is maximum.

Path difference = nλ
Phase difference = 2nπ

Destructive interference

Waves arrive in antiphase; resultant amplitude is minimum (zero if amplitudes are equal).

Path difference = (n + ½)λ
Phase difference = (2n + 1)π
相长：峰对峰；相消：峰对谷。相消不代表能量消失。

Young double-slit experiment

Interference of light

Pattern & equation

Two coherent slits act as sources. Their waves overlap at the screen, forming bright and dark fringes.

w = λD/a

w fringe spacing; λ wavelength; D slit-to-screen distance; a slit separation.

λ 或 D 增大 → 条纹间距增大；a 增大 → 条纹间距减小。

Measurement & assumptions

  • D ≫ a; small-angle approximation.
  • Measure centre-to-centre fringe spacing.
  • Measure several gaps, then divide by number of gaps.
10 fringe gaps: divide total measured length by 10, not by 11 bright lines.

Diffraction & grating

Wave spreading and sharp maxima

Diffraction

Diffraction is the spreading of waves as they pass through a gap or around an obstacle.

波长越大、缝越窄，扩散越明显；当 gap width 与 λ 相近时很明显。

Strong diffraction: gap width b ≈ λ

Single-slit diffraction

A narrow slit produces a broad central maximum and weaker side maxima.

sin θ = λ/b (first minimum)
small angle: θ ≈ λ/b
central angular width ≈ 2λ/b

Diffraction grating

Many equally spaced slits produce sharp principal maxima.

d sin θ = nλ

d grating spacing; n order; θ angle from normal.

d = 1/(lines per metre). Convert lines mm⁻¹ to m⁻¹ first.

Effect of changing variables

  • λ increases → pattern spreads.
  • Single-slit b decreases → central maximum wider.
  • Grating d decreases → same order has larger θ.
  • More slits → principal maxima narrower/sharper.

Stationary waves

Fixed nodes and antinodes

Formation

A stationary wave forms from two progressive waves of the same frequency, wavelength and amplitude travelling in opposite directions.

两列相同波反向传播并叠加，形成固定节点和腹点；图样不向前传播。

Nodes & antinodes

  • Node: zero displacement at all times.
  • Antinode: maximum amplitude.
  • Adjacent node–node / antinode–antinode = λ/2.
  • Node to nearest antinode = λ/4.
  • Within one loop points in phase; adjacent loops antiphase.

Progressive vs stationary

ProgressiveStationary
Transfers energyNo net energy transfer
Amplitude ideally sameAmplitude varies with position
No fixed nodesFixed nodes/antinodes

Strings & pipes

String / open pipe: L = nλ/2
fₙ = nv/(2L), n=1,2,3…

Closed pipe: node at closed end, antinode at open end; odd harmonics only.

L = (2n−1)λ/4

Resonance

Large amplitude at natural frequency

Definition & application

Resonance occurs when a system is driven at its natural frequency, producing a large amplitude.

驱动频率等于系统固有频率时，振幅显著增大。

Resonance tube method

For adjacent resonances in a closed tube:

L₂ − L₁ = λ/2
λ = 2(L₂ − L₁)

Use multiple resonance lengths to reduce uncertainty.

WAVE · Practice & worked solutions

Answer → method → exam takeaway

W1 · A wave has f = 12 Hz and λ = 0.25 m. Calculate its speed.
Show standard solution
Step 1 — Equation
v = fλ
Step 2 — Substitute
v = 12 × 0.25
Step 3 — Answer + unit
v = 3.0 m s⁻¹

Extension: if frequency doubles in the same medium, wavelength halves because v remains constant.

W2 · Two coherent waves have path difference 0.60λ. Find phase difference and state the type of interference.
Show standard solution
Step 1
Δφ = 2πΔx/λ
Step 2
Δφ = 2π × 0.60 = 1.20π rad = 216°
Step 3
Not exactly constructive or destructive; compare with 0, π, 2π. It is closer to antiphase than in-phase.

Exam insight: only nλ gives constructive and (n+½)λ gives destructive. Other path differences give partial reinforcement/cancellation.

W3 · In Young’s experiment λ=600 nm, D=1.5 m and a=0.30 mm. Calculate fringe spacing.
Show standard solution
Step 1 — Convert
λ=6.0×10⁻⁷ m; a=3.0×10⁻⁴ m
Step 2 — Formula
w=λD/a=(6.0×10⁻⁷×1.5)/(3.0×10⁻⁴)
Step 3 — Answer
w=3.0×10⁻³ m = 3.0 mm

Reasoning: increase D by 2 → w doubles; increase a by 2 → w halves.

W4 · Adjacent resonance lengths are 18.2 cm and 31.0 cm. Find wavelength and frequency if v=340 m s⁻¹.
Show standard solution
Step 1
ΔL=31.0−18.2=12.8 cm=0.128 m
Step 2
λ=2ΔL=0.256 m
Step 3
f=v/λ=340/0.256≈1.33×10³ Hz

Answer: λ=0.256 m; f≈1.33 kHz.

Charge & current

Flow of charge

Current

Current is the rate of flow of charge.

I = ΔQ/Δt　　Q = It

Conventional current is the direction positive charge would flow; electrons in metals move opposite.

电流按正电荷方向定义，金属电子反向运动。

Quantisation of charge

Q = Ne　　e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C

N is number of elementary charges. Charge is measured in coulombs (C).

Potential difference & power

Energy transferred per charge

Potential difference

Potential difference is energy transferred per unit charge.

V = W/Q　　W = VQ
电势差是每单位电荷转移的能量，单位 J C⁻¹。

Power & energy

P = IV = I²R = V²/R
E = Pt = VIt

Check which quantities are known; choose the most direct form.

Resistance & resistivity

Ohm’s law and material properties

Ohm’s law

R = V/I

For an ohmic conductor, current is directly proportional to p.d. if temperature and other physical conditions remain constant.

温度等条件不变时才满足欧姆定律；灯丝升温会使电阻增大。

  • Resistor: straight I–V through origin.
  • Filament lamp: curve as temperature rises.
  • Diode: mainly forward conduction.

Resistivity

R = ρL/A

ρ resistivity (Ω m); L length (m); A cross-sectional area (m²).

Longer wire → larger R; larger area → smaller R.

Circular wire: A=πr²=πd²/4. Do not confuse diameter and radius.

Circuit rules

Series, parallel, divider and Kirchhoff

Series & parallel

Series: Rtotal=R₁+R₂+…
Parallel: 1/Rtotal=1/R₁+1/R₂+…
Two parallel: R=R₁R₂/(R₁+R₂)

Series: same current; p.d. divides. Parallel: same p.d.; current divides.

Potential divider

Vout = Vin × R₂/(R₁+R₂)

Explain sensor output by linking resistance change to voltage ratio.

Kirchhoff’s laws

  • Current law: total current entering a junction = leaving (charge conservation).
  • Voltage law: e.m.f.s around a loop = potential drops (energy conservation).

节点处电荷守恒；闭合回路能量守恒。

Balanced Wheatstone bridge

No current through galvanometer; its terminals have equal potential.

R₁/R₂ = R₃/R₄

Only use the balance relation when the bridge is balanced.

EMF & internal resistance

Terminal p.d. under load

Definitions & equations

e.m.f. ε is energy supplied by source per unit charge. Internal resistance r is resistance inside the source.

ε = I(R+r)
Vterminal = ε−Ir = IR

电流流过内阻会产生 Ir 压降；端电压低于电动势。开路时 I≈0，V≈ε。

V–I graph

V = ε − Ir
y-intercept = ε
gradient = −r

Internal resistance is magnitude of negative gradient. Use a large triangle on best-fit line.

A negative gradient is expected: as current increases, terminal voltage decreases.

ELECTRIC · Practice & worked solutions

Full substitution and units

E1 · A charge of 240 C passes a point in 2.0 min. Calculate current.
Show standard solution
Step 1 — Convert time
t=2.0×60=120 s
Step 2 — Equation
I=Q/t=240/120
Step 3 — Answer
I=2.0 A
E2 · A 12 V supply is connected to 6.0 Ω. Find current and power.
Show standard solution
Current
I=V/R=12/6.0=2.0 A
Power
P=VI=12×2.0=24 W (or V²/R=144/6=24 W)
E3 · A wire has ρ=1.7×10⁻⁸ Ω m, length 2.0 m and area 0.50 mm². Find resistance.
Show standard solution
Step 1 — Convert area
A=0.50×10⁻⁶ m²
Step 2
R=ρL/A=(1.7×10⁻⁸×2.0)/(0.50×10⁻⁶)
Step 3
R=0.068 Ω
E4 · A cell has ε=1.50 V and r=0.25 Ω. At I=1.2 A, find terminal p.d.
Show standard solution
Step 1
V=ε−Ir
Step 2
V=1.50−(1.2×0.25)=1.20 V
Check
Internal drop Ir=0.30 V; ε=terminal V + internal drop.
E5 · V–I graph points: (0.5 A, 1.4 V) and (2.5 A, 0.9 V). Estimate internal resistance.
Show standard solution
Step 1 — Gradient
ΔV/ΔI=(0.9−1.4)/(2.5−0.5)=−0.5/2.0=−0.25 V A⁻¹
Step 2 — Interpret
gradient=−r → r=0.25 Ω

Extension: extrapolate to I=0 for ε; here ε≈1.525 V from the line.

Practical skills · shared toolkit

Planning / data / graph / evaluation

Planning

  1. Identify IV and DV.
  2. State control variables and how to maintain them.
  3. Describe apparatus and measurement steps.
  4. Repeat readings; mean; anomalous values.
  5. Suitable range and multiple data points.
  6. Safety risks and precautions.

Uncertainty

% uncertainty=(absolute uncertainty/value)×100%
  • Repeated data: half range estimates random uncertainty.
  • Add absolute uncertainties for sums/differences.
  • Add percentage uncertainties for products/quotients/powers.

Graph skills

  • Axes: quantity + unit; sensible scale.
  • Use much of graph area; plot accurately.
  • Best-fit line/curve; large gradient triangle.
  • Read intercept and give units.
  • Linearise where required by theory.

Evaluation

Write: limitation → effect on measurement → specific improvement.

Avoid “human error”. Explain parallax, reaction time, resolution, alignment or temperature drift and how to reduce it.

Wave practicals

  • Ripple tank: measure several wavefront gaps → λ; v=fλ.
  • Resonance tube: adjacent resonance ΔL=λ/2.
  • Young slits: measure multiple fringe gaps; w=λD/a.

Electrical practicals

  • Ammeter in series; voltmeter in parallel.
  • Check meter range, polarity and connections.
  • Internal resistance: vary load, record I and V, plot V against I.
  • Control temperature for Ohm’s law/resistivity.
